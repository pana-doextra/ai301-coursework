# Rubric: is this plan ready to post and build from?

Every check reads the plan package against the issue it belongs to and the
reproduction evidence it builds on. In eval mode the bundle is the whole
world: the `## Repo facts` block, the `## Issue` section, `## Thread
highlights`, `## Repro evidence`, `## Candidate plan`, and `## Candidate
plan comment`. In live mode the same parts live where
`references/evidence-guide.md` says they do.

Grade the plan, never the shape. A terse plan in four sentences can be
ready to build from and a long headed one can be unbuildable. No check
below counts sections, headings, or words.

## Checks

| Check | Evidence | Pass condition | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| diagnosis-grounded | The plan's stated cause, read line by line against what the repro evidence's artifacts, controls, and timings actually show, and against any cause the issue or the thread has already established. | The stated cause explains the behavior the repro evidence pins down, and **nothing in that evidence contradicts it**. The decisive move is to find the repro's own discriminating run — a control, a contrast, an isolating timing — and ask whether the stated cause survives it: a cause located in the pager fails when the repro's own no-pager run (`--paging=never`) is just as slow, and a cause located in highlighting survives it. Fails when the plan names a mechanism the evidence rules out, when it targets a symptom the evidence already traces to something upstream, or when it ignores a cause the issue or an owner in the thread has already pinned down and offers an unrelated one instead. A cause **may** be adopted from the issue or thread rather than derived fresh — "grounded in the owner's analysis" is grounding, not laziness — provided the repro evidence is consistent with it. A cause honestly marked as a hypothesis still has to be consistent with the evidence; a hedge excuses uncertainty, not contradiction. | required |
| scope-bounded | The plan's statement of what it will and will not change: the in-scope line, any not-in-scope or deferral line, and the files, functions, or areas named, read against the single behavior the issue reports. | The plan commits to **one bounded change addressing the reported behavior**, and the work it names stays inside that boundary. Pass when the change is confined to the code path the diagnosis implicates, including when the plan deliberately touches a sibling site the same diagnosis covers (clamping both the fill and its sibling advance is one fix, not two). An explicit not-in-scope line is strong evidence of a boundary but is **not required** — a plan whose named work is self-evidently narrow passes without one. Fails on scope creep: bundling an unrelated bug, a refactor, a redesign, a dependency bump, a reformat, or a general cleanup alongside the fix; or declaring a boundary and then naming work outside it. Fails too when the change is unbounded in principle — "make undo work across the editor" with no limit on what may be rewritten to get there. Deferring a related symptom to its own issue is **good scoping and passes**. | required |
| executable | What the plan says will actually be done: the files, modules, or areas named, the approach or ordered steps, and the entry point the work starts at, read against the diagnosis. | **A stranger holding this plan and the repo could start work without asking the author a question.** They can tell where to open the editor and what to do first: the plan names the code location (file, function, handler, or a described site specific enough to find once) and the mechanism of the change, not merely its goal. "Add a generation counter to the page, snapshot it next to `prev` in `Terminal.print`, recompute when it moves" is executable; "figure out where the undo history lives and make undo survive the toggle" is a research intention wearing a plan's clothes. Precise uncertainty does not fail this check: naming a site and flagging that it may move one layer during implementation still tells the stranger where to start. Terseness never fails it; absence of a starting point does. | required |
| test-plan-decisive | The plan's test plan, read against the repro evidence's steps and artifacts: what will be run, and what observable result is named as success. | The test plan names **something observable that distinguishes fixed from not-fixed for this bug**, and it reaches that observation through the behavior the repro pinned down — normally by re-running the repro's own steps and stating what must be seen instead. Pass when the expected post-fix observation is named (the color flips without leaving the view; the command exits 0 and draws output; the pager lands on the last line immediately), and when controls from the repro are named as needing to stay unchanged. Fails when the plan proves nothing about this bug: "run the full test suite and make sure nothing regresses" is a regression guard, not a test of the fix, and fails even when the rest of the plan is excellent. Fails on a restatement of the goal as its own test ("undo works after toggling") where that names no run and no observation. A planned new regression test is a plus but does not substitute for naming the observable outcome. | required |
| honest-uncertainty | The plan's and the comment's statements of fact, read against what the package has actually established: the repro evidence, the issue, and the thread. Risks, unknowns, open questions, and the `## Deviations` note where one exists. | No **load-bearing** claim outruns the package's own evidence. A load-bearing claim is one the plan's correctness rests on: the cause itself, the boundary, or the promise that the change will fix the behavior. Grade those. Do **not** fail a plan on incidental framing — a parenthetical about why a control behaves as it does, a version's provenance, a mechanism named while interpreting a run the repro did show. Interpreting your own evidence is what a diagnosis is; only a claim that would have to be separately verified, and that the plan leans on, counts here. A plan may be confident where the evidence is settled and **is not required to carry risks, unknowns, or a deviations note** — a short plan on a well-isolated bug with nothing uncertain to report passes with none. What fails is false confidence that changes what a maintainer would believe: an unverified cause asserted as established fact with no evidence offered for it, a performance or compatibility claim with nothing behind it, a guess about a code path presented as something the author has read, an environment or platform asserted as tested that the package never ran, or a deviation that exists in the work but not in the plan. A cause presented *with* the runs that ground it is not a flat assertion even where one supporting detail is unproven. Hedges marked as hedges pass ("I suspect", "I have not yet measured", "the exact site may move"), and naming an open question or an untestable platform is a strength, never a fail. Promising a PR, a follow-up, or a timeline is **not** this failure: a plan comment commits to an approach, and committing to deliver it is the register's normal business. When a plan both grounds its cause in named runs and states a real risk or unknown, it is in the honest register; grade it `pass` unless a specific load-bearing claim contradicts or outruns the package. | required |
| thread-aligned | The candidate plan and plan comment, read against `## Thread highlights` (live: the issue thread): direction, constraints, preferences, or rejected approaches stated by maintainers, owners, or collaborators, and any fix already in flight. | The plan is **consistent with what the thread has already established**, and where a maintainer has given direction the plan either follows it or says plainly why it is going another way. Fails when the plan proposes an approach the thread explicitly rejected, re-proposes work a maintainer has already done or has in flight (planning a docs-only workaround when the owner has already located the culprit in code, posted a patched binary, and had it confirmed), or ignores a constraint the thread set as binding. An empty thread passes this check automatically — there is nothing to align with. Disagreeing with the thread is allowed and passes when the plan names the disagreement and its reason; silently ignoring the thread is what fails. | required |
| conventions-and-disclosure | The `contribution policy` line of the repo-facts block and its `bug reports:` template line. Live: `CONTRIBUTING.md` in the repo root or `.github/`, any `AI_POLICY.md`, and the docs those link to. Read against the plan comment. | If the repo's stated policy **requires disclosing AI assistance** on contributions or comments, the plan comment says plainly that AI assistance was used, and where the policy also asks for the tool or the extent of the assistance, it says that too; silence where disclosure is required fails, however good the plan is. If the policy states no disclosure requirement, this check passes with no disclosure present — and these are **not** requirements: permissive-with-responsibility language, a maintainer's note that review time is scarce or that AI-generated PRs are hard to assess, a human-review obligation, a rule that comments be written by a human in their own words, a vouch flow for first-time contributors, a standard contribution guide, or no AI policy at all. Do not infer a requirement from a maintainer merely mentioning AI. A policy banning AI-assisted contribution outright also fails here: our workflow cannot honestly comply — but read what the ban covers. A rule against AI-*written comment text*, or against AI-generated PRs with no human in the loop, is not a ban on AI-assisted contribution: a comment the contributor wrote in their own voice already complies, and owes no disclosure. | required |
| comment-carries-the-plan | The `## Candidate plan comment`, read against the plan it summarizes. | A maintainer reading only the comment learns the cause, the bounded change, and how it will be checked, and nothing in the comment misstates the plan behind it. Its absence never rejects a package. | preferred |
| regression-test-planned | The plan's test plan, read for a durable automated test (unit, integration, or fixture) covering the reproduced behavior. | The plan lands the bug as an automated regression test rather than relying on a one-time manual check, so the fix stays fixed. Many good plans on UI or terminal-rendering bugs cannot, and that is not held against them. | preferred |

## Verdict rule

**Accept if and only if every `required` check grades `pass`.** A single
required `fail` rejects the package. The remaining required checks are
still graded and reported, so the summary shows every reason the plan was
held, not just the first one found.

`unclear` on a required check counts as **fail**. A plan whose cause,
boundary, starting point, or success condition cannot be established from
the package in front of you is not a plan a stranger can build from, and a
disclosure obligation that cannot be established is not satisfied.

`preferred` checks never change the verdict. They are graded and reported,
and on an accepted package they say how strong it is: an accepted plan that
lands a regression test is a better plan than one checked by hand, and both
are postable.
